## Lesson 2: From Idea to a Script AI Can Use

```
Welcome back. In this lesson, we're going to take a simple idea and turn it into a script that AI video tools can work with.

Here's the key insight: AI video tools work best from a short, visual script. Not a long, flowing paragraph — a short, visual one. One line per shot, written the way a director would think.

Let me show you what I mean. Let's say you're making a video for a local restaurant. Here's a bad script:

"This restaurant has amazing food and great service and you should come visit."

That's too vague. The AI doesn't know what to show. Here's a good script:

"Shot 1: A close-up of a sizzling plate of jollof rice, steam rising.
Shot 2: A hand pouring a cold drink into a glass with ice.
Shot 3: A smiling waiter setting the plate down on a table.
Shot 4: A wide shot of the restaurant interior, warm lighting, happy customers."

See the difference? Each line describes exactly what the camera should see. That's what the AI needs.

Now, let's write one together. Pick a real product or service — yours or a client's. It could be a hair product, a phone repair service, a bakery, anything.

Start with the hook. The first 3 seconds decide whether someone keeps watching. Your hook should be visual and intriguing. Not "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel" — that's boring. Instead, show something that makes people curious.

Then, the body. This is where you show the product in action. What does it look like? What problem does it solve? Who's using it?

Finally, the call to action. What do you want the viewer to do? Visit a place? Send a message? Click a link?

Here's a template you can use:

"Hook: [Visual that grabs attention]
Body: [2-3 shots showing the product in action]
CTA: [One clear action you want the viewer to take]"

Now, here's the difference between a prompt that gets a usable clip and one that gets a random, unusable one. A bad prompt is: "A person using a product." A good prompt is: "A young woman in a yellow dress applying lipstick in front of a bathroom mirror, soft natural lighting, close-up shot, 4K quality."

The more specific you are — the subject, the setting, the lighting, the camera angle — the better the result.

Your assignment for this lesson: write a 30-second script for a real product or service. Use the template. Make each line visual and specific. In lesson 3, we're going to take your script and generate your first AI video clips with it. See you there.
```

## Lesson 3: Generating Your First AI Video Clips

```
Welcome back. In this lesson, we're going to take your lesson 2 script and generate your first AI video clips.

I'm going to walk you through this using a current mainstream tool — Runway, Pika, Kling, or Google's Veo are all reasonable starting points. The workflow is the same shape across all of them, so even if you're using a different tool, this will apply.

Step 1: Open your tool of choice. Create a new project.

Step 2: Take the first line of your script — your hook — and paste it into the prompt box. Remember, the more specific your prompt, the better the result. Include the subject, the setting, the lighting, and the camera angle.

Step 3: Generate. Most tools will give you a few options. Pick the one that best matches your script.

Step 4: Repeat for each line of your script. Generate 3 to 4 short clips total.

Now, here's something important: your first generation probably won't be perfect. That's normal. The AI might get the lighting wrong, or the subject might look a little off. Don't panic.

Here's the trick: iterate on your prompt twice. Change one thing — the lighting, the camera angle, the subject's clothing — and generate again. Iterating on a prompt twice usually beats writing one long, perfect prompt from the start.

Let me show you an example. Let's say my prompt was "A woman applying lipstick in front of a mirror" and the result looked flat and boring. I'd change it to "A young woman in a yellow dress applying red lipstick in front of a bathroom mirror, soft natural lighting from a window, close-up shot, 4K quality." That second version is much more likely to give me a usable clip.

Also, keep your clips short. Most AI video tools generate clips that are 3 to 5 seconds long. That's perfect for social video, where attention spans are short.

Once you have your clips, save them all in one folder. In lesson 5, we're going to stitch them together in an editor. But first, in lesson 4, we're going to add a voiceover and captions to make your video feel finished. See you there.
```

```

Let's start with the voiceover. A natural-sounding AI voiceover makes a video feel professional in a way that silent clips never do.

Here's how to do it. Take your script — the same one you used to generate your clips — and paste it into a voice tool like ElevenLabs. ElevenLabs has a free tier that gives you enough credits to get started.

Choose a voice. ElevenLabs has dozens of voices — different accents, different tones, different ages. Pick one that matches the mood of your video. For a professional product video, you might want a clear, confident voice. For something more casual, a warmer, friendlier voice.

Generate the voiceover. Download the audio file. You'll import it into your editor in lesson 5.

Now, let's talk about captions. Captions are non-negotiable for social video. Most people watch with the sound off — on the bus, at work, in public. If your video doesn't have captions, you're losing most of your audience.

Here's the good news: you don't have to add captions manually. Most AI video tools and editors can auto-generate them from your voiceover. In CapCut, for example, you can auto-generate captions with one click. They're not always perfect — you'll need to proofread and fix any mistakes — but they save you hours of typing.

Here's a pro tip: make your captions big and bold. Use a font that's easy to read on a small phone screen. And use a contrasting color — white text with a black outline works well on any background.

So to recap: generate a voiceover from your script using ElevenLabs, auto-generate captions in your editor, proofread the captions, and make sure they're big and readable. In lesson 5, we're going to take all of this — your clips, your voiceover, your captions — and edit them together into a finished video. See you there.
```

## Lesson 5: Editing and Polishing

```
Welcome back. In this lesson, we're going to take everything you've made so far — your AI clips, your voiceover, your captions — and stitch them together into a finished video.

We're going to use CapCut. It's free, it works on Windows and Mac, and it does everything a beginner needs.

Step 1: Open CapCut and create a new project.

Step 2: Import your clips. Drag them into the timeline in the order of your script — hook first, then body, then call to action.

Step 3: Trim each clip. AI-generated clips often have a little extra at the beginning or end. Trim them so each clip starts and ends exactly where you want.

Step 4: Add your voiceover. Import the audio file from lesson 4 and drag it into the audio track. Line it up so the voiceover matches the visuals.

Step 5: Add your captions. If you haven't already, auto-generate them now. Proofread and fix any mistakes.

Step 6: Add basic pacing. This is where you make your video feel professional. Cut on the beat of the voiceover — when the speaker starts a new sentence, cut to the next clip. This keeps the video moving and holds attention.

Step 7: Add a title card. This is a simple text screen at the start of your video that tells the viewer what they're about to watch. Keep it short — 2 to 3 seconds.

Step 8: Add background music. CapCut has a free music library. Pick something that matches the mood of your video. Keep the volume low — the voiceover should be the loudest thing.

Step 9: Export. For TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts, export at 1080 by 1920 pixels — that's vertical, 9:16 aspect ratio. Export at 30 frames per second.

And that's it. You've just made a complete AI video from scratch. In the final lesson, we're going to talk about how to get paid for this skill — three realistic ways to start earning money with AI video creation. See you there.
```

## Recording Tips

- **Pace:** Speak naturally, not too fast. Pause between sections.
- **Tone:** Conversational, like explaining to a friend.
- **Length:** Each script is 3–5 minutes spoken. Don't rush.
- **Setup:** Record in a quiet room. Use your phone's voice memo app or a USB mic.
- **Format:** Save audio as MP3 or WAV. Save video as MP4 (1080×1920 for vertical).
